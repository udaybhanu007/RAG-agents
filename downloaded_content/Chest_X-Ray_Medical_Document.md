## What is a Chest X-ray?
A **chest X-ray** is a diagnostic imaging test that uses a small amount of ionizing radiation to produce images of the chest. It provides a quick and non-invasive way to visualize the **heart, lungs, blood vessels, airways, diaphragm, and chest bones**.

The examination is painless, typically takes only a few minutes, and is one of the most commonly ordered medical imaging tests worldwide.

## How is a Chest X-ray Performed?

- **Positioning:**
  - Most commonly done in a **posteroanterior (PA) view** (X-ray beam passes from back to front).
  - In bedridden patients, an **anteroposterior (AP) view** is taken.
  - Additional **lateral views** may be requested for more detail.

- **Process:**
  - Patient stands or lies still while the image is captured.
  - Breath is held for a few seconds to prevent motion blur.
  - Protective shielding is sometimes used to limit radiation exposure.

- **Duration:** Typically 5–10 minutes including preparation.

## What a Chest X-ray Report Contains
The report is written in sections by the radiologist:
1. **Patient Information**
   - Name, age, gender, medical record number, date, referring doctor.

2. **Clinical Indication**
   - Reason for the X-ray (e.g., “Cough and fever for 1 week” or “Rule out pneumonia”).

3. **Technique**
   - Type of view (PA, AP, lateral), portable or standard, any technical limitations.

4. **Findings**
   - Detailed observations of chest structures:
     - **Heart & Mediastinum:**
       - Normal heart size is < 50% of chest width (cardiothoracic ratio).
       - Look for enlargement, abnormal shape, displaced trachea, widened mediastinum (e.g., aortic aneurysm).
     
     - **Lungs & Pleura:**
       - Normal lung fields are dark (air-filled).
       - Look for **opacities** (infection, tumor, fluid), **hyperinflation** (emphysema, asthma), **collapse** (atelectasis).
       - Pleural abnormalities: effusion (fluid), thickening, or pneumothorax (air).

     - **Diaphragm:**
       - Should appear smooth and dome-shaped.
       - Abnormalities: elevated (due to paralysis, liver enlargement) or flattened (COPD).

     - **Bones:**
       - Ribs, clavicles, vertebrae visible.
       - Fractures, deformities, bone lesions.

     - **Soft Tissues & Devices:**
       - Subcutaneous emphysema (air under skin).
       - Pacemakers, catheters, endotracheal tubes, chest drains – checked for correct placement.

5. **Impression / Conclusion**
   - Concise summary of key findings.
   - Examples:
     - “Normal chest X-ray.”
     - “Right lower lobe pneumonia.”
     - “Left pleural effusion with underlying collapse.”
     - “Mild cardiomegaly.”

## Common Conditions Detected
- **Infections:** Pneumonia, tuberculosis, bronchitis.
- **Chronic Lung Disease:** COPD, emphysema, pulmonary fibrosis.
- **Cancer:** Lung nodules, masses, metastasis.
- **Cardiac Disease:** Heart enlargement, fluid overload, pulmonary edema.
- **Trauma:** Rib fractures, pneumothorax (collapsed lung), hemothorax (blood).
- **Occupational Lung Disease:** Silicosis, asbestosis.
- **Other:** Diaphragmatic hernia, scoliosis affecting chest.

## Why a Chest X-ray is Performed
Doctors may request a chest X-ray to:
1. **Diagnose symptoms** – unexplained cough, chest pain, fever, breathing problems.
2. **Monitor conditions** – progression of pneumonia, TB, chronic lung disease.
3. **Evaluate treatment response** – follow-up after antibiotics, surgery, or cancer therapy.
4. **Check injuries** – rib fractures, chest trauma.
5. **Device placement** – pacemaker leads, endotracheal tubes, central venous lines.
6. **Pre-operative screening** – before surgery, especially in patients with chronic illness.

## Benefits and Limitations
**Benefits:**
- Quick, inexpensive, widely available.
- Provides important diagnostic clues.
- Useful for emergency and routine evaluation.

**Limitations:**
- Uses radiation (though very low dose).
- Cannot show very fine details compared to CT scan.
- Some abnormalities may be missed if small or hidden.
- Cannot always determine the exact cause — may require CT, MRI, or lab tests.
